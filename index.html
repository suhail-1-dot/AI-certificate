Introduction to Quantity Surveying

Quantity surveying is an important part of the construction industry. A quantity surveyor helps manage construction costs from the early planning stage through to completion of a project.

The main purpose is to make sure that construction work is properly measured, estimated, priced and controlled.

What Does a Quantity Surveyor Do?

  • Prepare construction cost estimates.
  • Measure quantities of construction materials.
  • Prepare Bills of Quantities.
  • Assist with tendering and procurement.
  • Monitor project costs.
  • Manage variations and financial changes.
  • Assist with contract administration.

Construction Cost Planning

Cost planning helps a project team understand how much a construction project is expected to cost. The estimate can be updated as the design develops.

Measurement

Measurement is the process of determining the quantity of work or materials required for a construction project. Examples include concrete, brickwork, flooring, plastering and painting.

Bill of Quantities (BOQ)

A Bill of Quantities provides a structured description of construction work and its measured quantities. It can be used when obtaining prices from contractors.

Description Quantity Unit

Estimating Construction Costs

Construction estimates may include materials, labour, plant, equipment, subcontractor costs, overheads and other project expenses.

Procurement Methods

Different procurement methods can be used depending on the project. Common approaches include traditional procurement, design and build, and management contracting.

Tendering

Tendering allows contractors to submit prices for construction work. The project team can then evaluate the submissions based on price, technical requirements, experience and other factors.

Cost Control

Cost control involves monitoring actual project expenditure against the approved budget. Early identification of cost increases can help prevent major financial problems.

Variations

A variation is a change to the originally agreed construction work. Variations may affect quantities, materials, design requirements and cost.

Contract Administration

Quantity surveyors may assist with payment applications, valuations, variations, claims and other financial aspects of construction contracts.

Lifecycle Costing

Lifecycle costing considers costs over the useful life of a building, including construction, operation, maintenance and replacement costs.

Digital Technology

Modern quantity surveying increasingly uses digital tools such as Building Information Modelling (BIM), estimating software, spreadsheets and digital measurement systems.
